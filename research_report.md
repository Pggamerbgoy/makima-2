## Origins and Early Development

The origins of Google can be traced back to 1996 when Larry Page and Sergey Brin, then students at Stanford University, began working on a search algorithm called "BackRub." This early project was designed to analyze the web's link structure to determine the importance of individual web pages. The algorithm, which later became the foundation for Google's search engine, was developed with the assistance of Scott Hassan and Alan Steremberg [1].

In 1998, Page and Brin officially launched Google as a company, with the goal of creating a more efficient and effective search engine. Their initial focus was on improving the user experience of web searching by prioritizing relevant and high-quality results. The first version of Google Search was released in 1998, and it quickly gained popularity due to its superior performance compared to existing search engines such as AltaVista and Yahoo! [1].

## Expansion and Diversification

As Google's search engine gained traction, the company began to expand its product offerings beyond basic web search. This was followed by the launch of Google News in 2002, which provided users with a personalized news feed based on their interests and location [1].

In addition to these consumer-facing products, Google also invested heavily in research and development.
